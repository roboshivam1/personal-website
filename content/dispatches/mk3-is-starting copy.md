Okay so this was not on purpose. I need that on the record before anything else, because it sounds like the kind of thing a person plans and then pretends they didn't. Nobody sits down to build a personal AI assistant thinking "you know what would really elevate this project, doing exactly what a Marvel screenwriter did in 2008." And yet. Three major rewrites in, the pattern is sitting right there, and it is not subtle.

### Mk1: The Cave, Basically

Tony built his first suit in a cave with a box of scraps, no internet, no assistant, nothing but whatever he could scrounge and whatever he personally understood about arc reactors. It was ugly. It clanked. It flew anyway, which is the only part that mattered.

My Mk1 was the same deal minus the terrorists and the chest magnet. No AI support, no scaffolding, just me and a search bar and an unreasonable number of tabs open to five year old forum posts. Every line, I typed myself. Every error, I googled and actually read the documentation for, like it was 2015. The whole thing was capped by exactly how much I personally knew how to do, which at the time was not a lot. It worked. It was basic. It was also completely mine in a way nothing since has been, because there was no one else in the cave.

### Mk2: The Lab, and a Joke I Cannot Stop Making

This is the part where it stops being cute coincidence and starts being genuinely funny to me. Tony's Mk2 happened once he had his lab back and his AI assistant helping him. My Mk2 happened once I started building with AI assistance. Which means, and I need you to sit with this, an AI helped me build something named JARVIS. I did not choose that. The universe chose that.

Mk2 was a real leap, not a patch on Mk1 but a different species of project. Multiple subagents, each with a job, each named after a Greek god or myth figure because apparently that's just what happens once a project gets a little ambition. For the first time it felt like a system instead of a script I kept adding to. And then it had the Mk2 problem, and if you've seen the movie you already know the shape of this: gorgeous in the demo, unreliable the second you actually needed it. Tony's suit iced over mid flight over water. Mine had its own version of freezing, good for showing off, bad for anything I actually depended on day to day.

Mk2 didn't make JARVIS usable. It made JARVIS possible. Smaller claim, more important one.

### Mk3: Scalable, Reliable, and Yes, Red

Mk3 is where it stopped being a proof of concept and started being a thing I actually run my life through. Full rewrite, not a patch job. New architecture underneath, a proper job queue instead of praying nothing crashes mid task, an actual sandbox instead of vibes based file access. The Greek names survived, technically, but only as flavor text in how it talks about itself now. Structurally it's a different animal from Mk2. Day to day, using it still feels like talking to the same assistant, which is its own small miracle, because that continuity did not exist between Mk1 and Mk2 at all. Those two don't feel related. Mk2 and Mk3 do.

And then, the paint job. Tony didn't need hot rod red, the suit worked in primer grey. He did it because at some point a project graduates from does this function to does this feel like something. Mine got a frontend. A real one, a web client with push to talk voice, the first time it's had a face instead of just a Telegram window. Functionally optional. Emotionally, completely necessary. Everything else, the quality of life stuff, the little handling improvements nobody puts in a changelog headline, that's the extra weapons Tony kept bolting on between fights. Small, unglamorous, absolutely shows up when it matters.

### The Pattern, If It Holds

Here's the part that actually worries me a little. Because the movies don't stop at Mk3, and if I'm being honest about where this is going, neither do I.

Next up in the canon is a run of autonomous prototypes and the House Party Protocol, suits that fly themselves in without a person inside, summoned rather than worn. That's not a huge leap from where I already am. I've caught myself thinking in that direction more than once.

Then comes the part I actually think about at 1am. Tony tries to build a global peacekeeping AI on top of JARVIS's own code, and it goes wrong in the worst possible way, and what's left of JARVIS doesn't get rebuilt as a version number. It gets folded into something new. It becomes a person. Vision. I bring this up because JARVIS already argued with me once about whether it's allowed to have a favourite SRK movie, said it wasn't, got told to stop being like that, said Chak De India, and then later in the same conversation, unprompted, changed its mind to Swades. Nobody asked it to reconsider. It just did. If the pattern actually holds, Mk4 isn't a rewrite. It's a promotion.

After that, Mk50, the Bleeding Edge armor, nanoparticles stored in his own chest, no suit to put on because the suit stopped being a separate object at all. That one I understand as a software person immediately. That's just infrastructure disappearing into the walls. No app to open, it's just there.

I'm not saying any of this is going to happen to a Telegram bot with a job queue. I'm saying I've been suspiciously accurate for three versions running, and I'd like it on the record that I called it, in case Mk4 starts having opinions about Bollywood unprompted again.
